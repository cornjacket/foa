# Friday 8/21 — 120 min

**Sidelines 0–30, half court 30–120 — and every scrimmage tonight starts with a called
offense.** The half-court block alternates drill → game, drill → game, three times: learn the
action, then immediately play a game where running it is the cheapest way to score. Nothing
gets installed cold.

**Tonight is the first live test of calling the offense.** The PG says one word crossing
mid-court — **3-Out, SL, or SR** — and everybody moves off it. No numbers, no colors, no hand
signals: the word carries the meaning, so there's nothing to decode under pressure. Full
explanation of what each call obligates: [the call](#the-call--3-out-sl-sr).

**Two constraints ride every scrimmage and don't retire tonight:** the offense has to *spread*
on any dead-ball inbound, and the PG has to *call* something crossing mid-court. A possession
that starts silent is a turnover. Everything else — box-out penalty, inbound plays — layers on
top one game at a time.

**Substitutions run off the last bucket. Track who scored last.** See
[substitutions](#substitutions--the-last-bucket-is-the-sub).

## The call — 3-Out, SL, SR

**The PG calls the offense out loud as he crosses mid-court, before the defense is set.** One
word, one time, loud enough that everyone hears it. The word *is* the instruction — that's why
it's a word and not a color or a number.

**Three calls. That's the entire book tonight.**

- **3-Out** *(3v3v3 only)* — neutral. Three across the perimeter, paint empty, no side declared.
  Read the defense and play: DHO, screen away, whatever they give you.
- **SL** — strong side left. The ball goes left and the action happens left.
- **SR** — strong side right. Mirror image.

**SL/SR declares the side, not the play.** The side is what everybody needs in advance —
where to stand, who's the outlet, which way the ball reverses. What happens *inside* the side
is still a read.

### Spread on the inbound

Every dead ball under the basket restarts the same way: **bunch at the key, then spread to the
free-throw line extended, crossing sides on the way out.** The right-side man finishes left.
A defender has to track a man who's already moving, and it hides who's cutting. The safety
cheats to the ball side before the slap — shorter pass, and he's the release when the count
gets long.

In 3v3v3 it's the same shape with three bodies: two spread and cross, one safety strong side.

### 4v4 — SL/SR into 5-Out or Motion

In 4v4 the fifth spot is just empty. **Same 5-Out spacing, one fewer body** — everybody on the
perimeter, nobody in the paint, and the empty spot is a driving lane rather than a hole.

**Alignment on SL** *(SR is the mirror)*:

- PG with the ball at the **top-left slot**
- **Wing and corner filled on the left** — that's the strong side
- Fourth man on the **right wing** — weak-side release and the skip target

Both calls start from that exact alignment. What separates them is the rule the PG follows
after the entry pass:

**5-Out — pass and follow.** Entry goes to the strong-side wing and the **PG follows his own
pass into a DHO**. Corner lifts behind it to keep the spacing. Finishes: layup off the
handoff, elbow jumper, or the skip to the weak-side man when the help rotates over.

**Motion — pass and screen away.** Entry goes to the strong-side wing, then the **PG screens
away** for the weak-side man, who fills the top. Ball reverses through the new top man and the
pattern runs back the other direction. Nobody stands still, and the possession doesn't end
until somebody's open.

**How they pick — the defense picks for them.**

- **Ball pressure, defender sitting on the handoff → Motion.** Screening away gets the ball
  out of trouble and makes them chase.
- **Soft coverage, defender trailing → 5-Out.** Take the handoff downhill; nobody's there to
  meet it.

That's the read. Call the side; let them solve the side.

## Substitutions — the last bucket is the sub

**Keep one fact in your head all night: who scored last.**

- **At the next dead ball, the last scorer and the man guarding him come off.** Two fresh
  bodies go on. Nobody has to be singled out and the game never stops for a lecture.
- **Back-to-back buckets by the same player → he comes off immediately**, dead ball or not.
  The game stops being about one kid, and two players who were watching get the floor.
- **A penalty is also a substitution.** The player who broke the rule comes off; the free
  throw is when I tell him *which rule he broke*, not how to beat it. He goes back in at the
  next stoppage once he's said the rule back to me.
- 9 athletes: **3v3v3 is three fixed teams of three, 4v4 leaves one off.** In the 3v3v3 blocks
  the waiting team is the substitution — scorer's team stays, the other comes off.
