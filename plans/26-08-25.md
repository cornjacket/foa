# Tuesday 8/25 — 120 min

**Full court 0–60, half court 60–90, sidelines 90–120.** We have the whole floor for the first
hour, so the full court gets the hour and everything that needs it goes there. **No warm-up
block up front — the hour is four 15-minute blocks and nothing else.** Dribbling and
stretching run in the sidelines block at the end, where they don't cost us floor time.

**Three drills, then the press pair runs backwards.** Free throws first while every basket in
the gym is free — it's also the currency every defensive penalty pays out in tonight, so the
rep and the stakes are the same thing. **It's the only easy block on the floor, so get them
moving on their own before the first ball goes up** — the Hail Mary is a full-speed sprint
twenty minutes in, and it feeds straight into the pressed game.

**The Hail Mary has two static defenders and no live ones.** The man on the passer and the
safety at mid-court don't contest anything — they're there so the ball has to leave high and
stay high. Two pairs run it at once, which means **the lanes are the safety rule: nobody
crosses into the other pair's path**, and a rep gets waved off rather than letting two
sprinting kids converge on the same ball.

Then they play a pressed game **cold**, find out what it costs, and *only then* do we drill
the break. The fix lands on a problem they've already felt instead of one I described, and
it's the last thing they do on the full court — so the answer is the freshest thing in their
heads when they leave the floor.

**Friday's calls carry over and don't retire.** The PG calls the offense — **3-Out, SL, SR** —
crossing mid-court, and a silent possession is a turnover. What's new tonight is that the ball
has to *survive* a press to get there. [Recap of the calls](#the-call--3-out-sl-sr).

## Constraint bank — written, not in play tonight

Rules that are ready to go but aren't live yet. Adding one is a decision, not an afterthought
— the reason there's a cap is that past three or four rules they stop playing and start
trying to remember. **Pull one or two, never a list.**

**A constraint retires the moment they beat it without thinking about it** — and it comes back
here marked mastered, it doesn't get deleted. **The bank is supposed to grow.** A long list
means the habits underneath it are owned and we're free to go work on the next one.

**Next week: no dribbling into the corner, and no dribble above the free-throw line
extended.** Both are dribble rules and they point the same direction — the ball moves by pass
until somebody actually has an advantage. Running them together is one idea with two edges,
not two things to remember.

**Offense — the penalty is a turnover**

- **No dribbling into the corner** — **going in next week.** The corner is where a dribble
  goes to die: the baseline and the sideline do the defense's work for it, and a trap down
  there has no way out.
- **Dribble it down and pull a 3 → the basket doesn't count and they lose the ball.** Even if
  it goes in. It's the worst shot we take: no pass, no advantage, nobody in position to
  rebound it. **Harsher than the rest on purpose** — a make that still costs you the
  possession is the only way to stop a shot that gets rewarded once in six tries.
- **The ball has to touch the paint before a shot goes up** — kills the settle-for-a-jumper
  possession and creates the kickout.
- **Everybody touches it before a shot** — nobody can hide, and the ball-dominant kid has to
  find people.
- **No dribble above the free-throw line extended** — **going in next week.** Out on the
  perimeter the ball moves by **pass only**. Below that line you can dribble all you want, so
  **drives are still legal** — what dies is standing at the top bouncing it while four people
  watch.
- **Two passes after an offensive rebound before you shoot** — turns a scramble into a reset
  instead of a wild put-back.
- **The ball has to reach the other side of the floor before anyone shoots** — right wing,
  through the top, to the left wing. Possessions live on one side at this age, which lets the
  two defenders on the weak side stand in the lane as free help all day. Reversing it makes
  them sprint out and close out late — that's where the advantage comes from, not the drive.
- **Score off two feet in the paint** — no running one-foot floaters; gets them balanced and
  drawing contact.

**Defense — the penalty is a free throw and the offense keeps the ball**

- **Call the screen out loud, every time** — silence is the foul.
- **No reaching** — feet and chest, hands off. Cheapest fouls we give up.
- **Talk on every pass** — "ball," "help," "I got him." If nobody said anything, it's a
  penalty even if the stop was good.
- **Match up before the ball crosses half court** — no jogging back and sorting it out late.
- **The man who helped has to recover to his own shooter** — help is not a place to stay.
- **Contest with a hand up, without leaving your feet** — pump fakes stop working.
- **Nobody scores twice on the same guy in a row** — his defender has to fix it himself or
  the team pays a free throw.

## The call — 3-Out, SL, SR

**The PG calls the offense out loud as he crosses mid-court, before the defense is set.** One
word, one time, loud enough that everyone hears it.

- **3-Out** — neutral. Three across the perimeter, paint empty, no side declared. Read the
  defense and play.
- **SL** — strong side left. The ball goes left and the action happens left.
- **SR** — strong side right. Mirror image.

**SL/SR declares the side, not the play.** What happens inside the side is still a read — full
4v4 breakdown of 5-Out and Motion off the call is in
[Friday's plan](26-08-21.md#4v4--slsr-into-5-out-or-motion).

## Substitutions — the last bucket is the sub

**Keep one fact in your head all night: who scored last.**

- **At the next dead ball, the last scorer and the man guarding him come off.** Two fresh
  bodies go on. Nobody has to be singled out and the game never stops for a lecture.
- **Back-to-back buckets by the same player → he comes off immediately**, dead ball or not.
- **A penalty is also a substitution.** The player who broke the rule comes off; the free
  throw is when I tell him *which rule he broke*, not how to beat it. He goes back in at the
  next stoppage once he's said the rule back to me.
- 9 athletes: **4v4 leaves one off, 3v3 leaves three off.** The odd man goes to the stronger
  side, so I have a bench on that side only and the weaker team never leaves the floor. In the
  3v3 press game the three off are the next team on — rotate them in on the last bucket.
- **The 5v5 press break has no bench** — nobody subs, everybody gets every rep. Swap offense
  and defense instead.
