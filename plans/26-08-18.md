# Tuesday 8/18 — 120 min

**New format: practice is a scoreboard, not a drill sheet.** Teams get picked in the first
five minutes and stay fixed all night. Every block is a game, the score carries from block to
block, and the coaching happens through the rules — add a constraint, let them figure out how
to beat it, and let the ones who don't pay for it in possessions. Drills still exist, but each
one now sits *between* two games: learn it, then immediately play a game where it's the
cheapest way to score.

**Full court 0–60, half court 60–105, sidelines 105–120.**

## How the games run

**Two rules and a bonus — that's the cap.** One thing the offense can lose the ball for, one
thing the defense gives up a free throw for, and one way to score double. Any more than that
and they stop playing and start trying to remember. A constraint retires the moment they beat
it without thinking about it.

**Teams.** Two fixed teams — 4v4 on the full court, 3v3 in the last half-court game. **The odd
player goes to the stronger team.** That gives me a bench on that side only: I can pull the
best player at any time without singling anybody out, and the weaker team never leaves the
floor. That's the whole design — the players who need scrimmage reps get every minute of it,
and the player who'd otherwise dominate gets rationed by the format instead of by me.

**Scoring — one running total, all practice.**

- Bucket = **1**
- Bucket that satisfies the block's live constraint = **2**
- Free throw off a defensive penalty = **1**

Call the total out loud between every block. Somebody wins the night.

**Penalties.**

- **Offense breaks its rule → turnover.** Dead ball, other team's ball.
- **Defense breaks its rule, or fouls → one free throw, and the offense keeps the ball.**
  Free throws are the rep we're shortest on anyway, so the penalty pays us twice.
- **Every penalty is also a substitution.** See below — the whistle is the only free time we get.

**Substitutions run on dead time, not on the game clock.** Every stoppage we've already paid
for gets spent putting a fresh body on the floor.

- **A penalty is a substitution.** The player who broke the rule comes off and the next one
  goes on. **The free throw is when I talk to him** — the shooter's at the line anyway, so the
  explanation costs the game nothing and the other seven keep playing. It isn't punishment —
  it's the only quiet twenty seconds in the gym, and it's why the game never stops for a
  lecture.
- **He goes back in at the next stoppage, once he's told me the rule back.** The sit is the
  conversation, not the sentence. **Break the same rule twice and he stays out longer** — by
  then it isn't a misunderstanding.
- **Back-to-back buckets by the same player → he comes out, and so does his defender.** The
  game stops being about one kid, and two players who were watching get the floor.
- **Coach's horn** — the odd man on the strong team swaps in whenever I want him to.

**Why the rule-break sub matters more than it looks.** On Saturday there's a ref, and I can't
add a constraint to make anybody run the offense — the only lever left is who's on the floor.
A constraint here is the same thing as a formation or a trigger there: **know it, run it, stay
in the game.** Players who freelance find out what that costs on a Tuesday, when the cost is
sixty seconds and a conversation instead of a possession in a real game.

**What I do during a game: call the rule and call the score. Not the solution.** Thirty-second
huddle between games — the team fixes it themselves, out loud, and I listen instead of talk.
**The sideline conversation is the same deal:** I tell him *which rule he broke*, not how to
beat it. Knowing the constraint is on him; solving it is still theirs.

## Constraint bank

For future practices — pull one offense rule, one defense rule, and one bonus. Never more.

**Offense — penalty is a turnover**

- Three passes before a shot / everybody touches before a shot
- The ball has to touch the paint before a shot goes up
- No dribble above the free-throw line extended
- Every possession runs a named action (DHO, Zoom, ball screen, screen away)
- Cross half court inside 5 seconds; inbound inside 5

**Defense — penalty is a free throw for the offense**

- Call the screen out loud, every time
- No reaching
- Match up before the ball crosses half court
- Help comes from the gap, and the man who helped recovers to the shooter
- Box out — an uncontested offensive rebound is the foul

**Bonus — worth 2**

- Scored in transition, before the defense is set
- Scored off the day's action
- Scored off a kickout after a paint touch
- Scored off a broken press
- Scored by the player who's been on the floor longest

**Routines**

- Between the legs
- Under drags
- Cross jabs
- Strongside spins
- Weakside spins
- Hips to drop step
- Speed stops
- Wrap around

**Combos on the move**

- Speed stop → Between the legs
- Speed stop → Between the legs → Wrap around
- Speed stop → Behind the back
